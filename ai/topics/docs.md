# Documentation

Trigger: editing under `docs/`.

The `docs/` directory holds **pedagogical material** for Kio — tutorials, topic guides, and host integration guides built on top of `specs/`. These are derivative and reader-facing, not contracts; `specs/` and the implementation remain the source of truth.

The current layout is one cluster per kind under `docs/`: `tutorials/` for learning-oriented walkthroughs, `guides/` for topic- and task-oriented pieces, `poc/` for case studies, `hosts/` for per-host integration guides. The catalog of specific files is [`docs/README.md`](../../docs/README.md).

**`docs/` is a standalone introduction to Kio.** A reader who starts at `docs/README.md` and follows its recommended order should understand most of the language and its ordinary development workflow without needing the specifications, source code, tests, blog history, agent guidance, or an external explanation. “Most” includes the central concepts and tools needed for everyday Kio work; it does not require teaching every specification edge case or repeating every host-specific detail. Concepts must be introduced before later documents rely on them. The specifications remain the authority for precision and edge cases, but a link to a specification must not substitute for the explanation a learner needs.

**Internal links must set accurate expectations.** When one document under `docs/` describes another document it links to, the description, destination, and any reading order around that link must agree about what the destination contains, who it is for, and when it should be read. None of those is automatically authoritative: resolve a mismatch by making the documentation truthful and the standalone learning path coherent. When a destination's scope, audience, or prerequisites change, review every document that describes or sequences it; a changed description or sequence may instead reveal that the destination needs revision. Do not fix a mismatch merely by weakening or deleting a meaningful description: first preserve the useful coverage or routing obligation somewhere appropriate, then re-evaluate the linking document's own explanation, prerequisites, and order. Apply the obligation according to each document's role: catalogs and routing pages direct readers accurately, sequences preserve prerequisite order, and standalone pages fulfill their advertised scope. The resulting structure must not overstate coverage, omit a prerequisite, or send a reader to the wrong kind of material.

**Tutorials are read top-to-bottom by someone new and are sequenced.** Each section builds on the prior. The reader's mental model grows as they progress.

**Guides are topic-organized and consumed by someone who already knows the language.** Each guide stands alone; readers come for the specific topic and leave without needing to read the others. A guide either introduces the prerequisites needed for its topic or links to the place in the learning path that does.

**Case studies (`docs/poc/<x>.md`) are reference-grade read-throughs of a single proof-of-concept package.** Each `docs/poc/<x>.md` walks [`test-data/poc/<x>/`](../../test-data/poc/) end to end: it covers the package's whole public surface, explains how the pieces fit, and quotes the package's `equiv` laws. The purpose is to be the *complete* picture of a body of Kio — the thing a topic guide isolates a slice of — and the re-home for material that is per-library semantics rather than language contract (the coercion-elaborator palettes' per-form rules live in [`docs/poc/elab.md`](../../docs/poc/elab.md), not in `specs/`). Two norms set case studies apart from the rest of `docs/`:

- **`docs ⊆ POC`.** A case study is **bounded by its backing package**: every type, function, rule, and law it shows must exist in `test-data/poc/<x>/` verbatim. A case study never documents behavior the POC does not exercise, and never reshapes the POC's source to read more nicely — if the POC is wrong, fix the POC (it is `kio test`-checked and run on every CI run), not the prose. The two move together; see § Keeping a case study paired with its POC.
- **Precision over plain-English-only.** The plain-English-default rule below is **relaxed for case studies**: a case study may be as technically precise as the POC it documents, including PLT vocabulary (DNF, variance, residual normal form, initial-object law) where the POC's behavior genuinely turns on it. Case studies are read by someone who already knows the language and wants the exact contract a package commits to. Still prefer a plain-English gloss when one is as precise, and still link a load-bearing term to where it is defined.

**Audience: developers without a PLT background.** Default to plain English. Programming-language-theory jargon — "residual normal form", "β-reduction", "let-unfolding", "fn-call inlining", "partial evaluation", "discharge", "type scheme", "rank-N", "open-world monotonicity", and the like — is appropriate in `specs/`, where the audience reads the formal contract. In `docs/` it raises the floor on who can read the material without giving them an exit. Prefer the plain-English description ("the REPL simplifies as far as it can"); when a technical term is load-bearing, introduce it inline and link to wherever it's defined. The audience is someone using Kio to write a program, not someone working on the compiler.

**Relevance is part of altitude, not just readability.** Prose can clear the plain-English bar and still be wrong for `docs/` when it answers a question the reader never asked — the test is *why should a user care?* A host guide (`docs/hosts/<lang>.md`) is for someone embedding Kio in one language: it must not editorialize about where its backend sits among Kio's backend families, contrast it against other backends' internal machinery (erasure, brand markers, carrier walks), or narrate compiler-internal structure the reader has no referent for. What a host author needs is *what they see and do at the boundary*; where the backend sits in the taxonomy is a link to [`specs/backends/README.md`](../../specs/backends/README.md) § Language families, never editorial prose.

**Host guides do not version or grade backends.** A shipping backend is presumed to implement the full Kio contract, so `docs/hosts/<lang>.md` carries no backend-specific version, maturity tier, “experimental” label, or “may evolve” banner. It does carry exactly one `Host API stability: evolving|stable` field immediately below the title, formatted like the backend pages and matching the corresponding backend spec, followed by an adjacent link to [`specs/backends/README.md` § Host API stability](../../specs/backends/README.md#host-api-stability). That field is compatibility policy, not a quality or conformance grade. If a concrete known caveat affects that host, state it in a top banner that matches the corresponding `specs/backends/<lang>.md` banner. Name the actual breakage and the host action it requires; a vague warning is not a caveat.

Snippets in `docs/` use **Kiodoc**: GitHub-flavored Markdown plus a small set of fence-attribute directives for runnable / validatable Kio snippets. The directive contract is specified in [`specs/kiodoc.md`](../../specs/kiodoc.md); the `kio doc` subcommand (see [`specs/cli.md`](../../specs/cli.md) § `kio doc`) validates every non-`{ignore}` `kio` fence in `docs/` on every CI run, gating merges on snippet failures.

`kio doc` validates only the `kio` fences. The **host-language** fences on a `docs/hosts/<lang>.md` page — the `java` / `go` / `swift` / … code a host author copies — are owned by [`ci/checks/orchestrators/host-docs-snippets.sh`](../../ci/checks/orchestrators/host-docs-snippets.sh): for each page it materializes the page's own example package from its `{file}` / file-backed-harness fences, `kio build`s it, and compiles every host fence against the freshly-built facade (wrapping a statement-level snippet only in scaffolding the page itself shows). A host fence that stops compiling — because the emitter renamed a facade symbol the page still spells the old way — fails the run. Editing a `docs/hosts/` page's host snippets, or the facade an emitter emits, puts that check on the hook; each page's `kio` fences must therefore also assemble into one buildable package (a `{file}` support set plus a file-backed-harness `main`, not `{ignore}`).

Do **not** use `{ignore}` as the default for examples that are inconvenient to set up. A `kio` fence should normally be validated, and `{ignore}` should appear only in rare circumstances. Before ignoring a `kio` fence, prefer a hidden or visible harness, a document-scoped `{file}` fence with a file-backed harness when the snippet needs neighboring files, `variant=KIND`, `check_exit_code=N` for negative examples whose expected failure is stable, and snippet placeholder substitution (`placeholder={"...":"valid_replacement()"}`) when the visible example needs display-only syntax but a nearby valid replacement can still check the surrounding code. If an example is only a grammar shape or pseudocode, use prose, a table, inline code, or a non-Kio fence such as `text` instead of a skipped Kio fence. `{ignore}` is appropriate only for true fragments, clearly labeled negative examples where validating the failure would obscure the point, or snippets that still cannot be made meaningful under Kiodoc; `variant=KIND ignore` is acceptable for skipped file-kind fragments.

**Keep `docs/` consistent with `specs/` and the implementation.** When a change in behavior is described in `docs/`, update the affected docs in the same change. `specs/` and the implementation are the source of truth; if `docs/` and `specs/` diverge, `docs/` is the bug. `docs/` may lag in *coverage* but never in *correctness*.

**Public docs outside the blog link only public docs.** Outside `docs/blog/*.md`, `docs/` must not send readers into the agent-guidance layer (`AGENTS.md`, `ai/topics/`, `ai/skills/`). If a concept is useful to users, link to `specs/`, `docs/`, `README.md`, `TESTING.md`, or `CONTRIBUTING.md`; if it is only agent guidance, omit it.

## Blog posts

**Blog post bodies are the authorial exception.** The no-leak rule does not apply to hand-written `docs/blog/*.md` bodies, and a post may link to any tracked repository path, including agent guidance. A GitHub link to a repository file or directory must pin the target with a full commit object ID (`blob/<commit>/…` or `tree/<commit>/…`), never a branch or tag such as `main`. Blog post edits remain approval-gated by `AGENTS.md`.

**Blog examples use current Kio.** The docs package's recursive Kiodoc CI checks
include blog pages. Their examples must meet their declared snippet expectations
with the current compiler, even when the surrounding prose discusses history.
For a focused review, use the [`review-blog-post`](../skills/review-blog-post/SKILL.md)
skill's check-only commands and report concrete compilation or formatting fixes.
Hidden harnesses may supply genuine omitted context, but remain protected post
content; visible code stays hand-written. A CI failure does not authorize editing
either, ignoring the example, or preserving language compatibility by default.
Bring a language/post conflict and its alternatives to the maintainer for a
decision instead of silently changing the post or adding a compatibility shim.

**Blog posts form a chronological communication record.** Read them oldest to newest. A checkable factual claim about Kio remains active unless it is explicitly scoped to a date or release, or a later post clearly retracts, corrects, or supersedes it. A newer conflicting statement does not silently replace the earlier communication; without an explicit acknowledgement it is a contradiction. Dated and release-scoped claims are judged against that historical state, while unqualified and timeless claims must remain true of the current repository. Personal opinion, rhetoric, hopes, plans, and predictions are not factual claims merely because later events differ. [`audit-blog-history`](../skills/audit-blog-history/SKILL.md) maintains this claim-and-retraction ledger across the complete post sequence.

## Backend-independent tutorials and guides

**`docs/tutorials/` and `docs/guides/` are backend-agnostic.** They describe the language and its surface forms — what a Kio program *means*, not how a particular backend lowers it. Backend-specific material (per-backend struct emission, runtime adapter shape, language-version floor, FFI value layout, name mangling) is the wrong fit for these directories. Where it belongs depends on audience: contract-level surface for host authors goes in [`specs/backends/<lang>.md`](../../specs/backends/); pedagogical walkthroughs for someone integrating a host go in `docs/hosts/<lang>.md`. A guide that needs to mention a backend by name in passing (e.g., "the `js` backend erases types at runtime") is fine — that is spec-level behavior; what doesn't belong is anything keyed to a backend's emitted artifacts or runtime internals.

## Keeping a case study paired with its POC

A `docs/poc/<x>.md` case study and its backing `test-data/poc/<x>/` package are a **pair**: each must stay in sync with the other.

- **Every `docs/poc/<x>.md` has a backing `test-data/poc/<x>/`.** A case study with no POC is an orphan — delete it or write the POC.
- **`docs ⊆ POC`.** Nothing in a case study may be absent from its POC: every type, function, rule, operator, and `equiv` law shown must exist in the package verbatim. The POC is the ground truth (it is `kio check` + `kio test` + per-backend-`main`-run checked on every CI run); the prose is derivative.
- **The POC's headline public surface is covered.** A case study should walk the package's whole public API and operator DSL, not a convenient subset. A `pub` item the POC exports but the case study never mentions is a coverage gap.
- **Reusable libraries have a usable adoption path.** A guide or case study presenting a POC as a library must provide, or link directly to, a complete `.dep.kio` example or shared template with explicit library-specific values: consumer filename/alias, repository/ref and actual package-manifest path, required host bindings, and representative imports. Show `kio dep fetch` from the consumer package root; link shared lock/materialized-source explanations to the [using-libraries guide](../../docs/guides/using-libraries.md). Consumer setup is usage, not a quotation of library source. Validate the syntax, expected repository/ref, and local package/import/binding relationships. Documentation-only adoption examples need no fetch test; changes to fetch/materialization behavior need a hermetic fixture. Syntax and local-value checks do not prove fetching from the published repository works, and a local fixture must not be reported as a remote-fetch test.
- **They move together.** When the POC changes (a new combinator, a renamed type, a changed law), update the paired case study in the same change; when the case study claims a behavior, the POC must exercise it. This is the same lockstep discipline `specs/` and the implementation hold, applied to the `docs/poc/` ⇄ `test-data/poc/` pair. The [`audit-poc-docs-sync`](../skills/audit-poc-docs-sync/SKILL.md) skill sweeps for violations.
